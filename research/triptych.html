Related reading

  1. Reber, Schwarz & Winkielman · 2004 · Processing fluency and aesthetic pleasure ↗This paper looks at how features such as repetition and figure–ground contrast relate to aesthetic responses. It offers a way to think about the shared outlines and lettering, without prescribing how the work should feel.
  2. Wolfe & Horowitz · 2017 · Five factors that guide visual search ↗Wolfe and Horowitz explain that attention depends on meaning and previous experience as well as visual contrast. That is why the viewer’s context matters here.

These notes describe the artwork and possible ways of reading it. The papers cannot tell us what a particular viewer will feel or whether they will share the artist’s interpretation.

Interviews to run next

Who to speak to

Adult viewers with different familiarity with Ukrainian visual culture and this historical context. Do not recruit on an assumption that nationality determines a reading.

Questions for individual interviews · IDI

  1. Before I explain the project, what do you notice?
  2. Which details make these images feel connected or separate?
  3. What do the colour fragments and handwriting suggest to you?
  4. What changed when you saw all three together rather than one panel?

Tasks to try

  1. View one panel and describe what is visible before interpreting it.
  2. View the three together and identify any links.
  3. Read the artist’s context statement, then explain what changed and what remained ambiguous.

Keep what people describe separate from what they think it means. Note how their reading changes after hearing the context. Agreement with the artist is not the goal of the interview.

Topics for a group discussion · FGI

Optional: two groups of 4–6 adult viewers with varied familiarity. Collect private interpretations first, then discuss how meanings differ. Allow passing on any question. Analyse group influence and disagreement instead of counting a consensus as truth.

About this review

The review uses the full case-study text, the design files shown in the portfolio and the published research listed above. Each coded fragment keeps the observation separate from its interpretation. The notes describe the design and what to test next; they do not report how real users responded.
